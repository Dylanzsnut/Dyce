### Turn 1
**User Query:**
> We have an outdoor awards gala for 300 guests scheduled tomorrow night at 7:00 PM. The forecast reports a 45% chance of severe thunderstorms between 6:00 PM and 9:00 PM. Option 1: Tent the lawn for $6,500 (requires 5-hour advance notice, deadline 1:00 PM). Option 2: Move into the indoor backup ballroom for $2,000 flip fee, but max ballroom capacity is 260. Walk through your risk-mitigation reasoning step-by-step and provide an action timeline.

**Assistant Response (Chain-of-Thought / Sentiment / Context):**

### Step-by-Step Risk Mitigation & Decision Framework:

1. **Step 1: Risk Assessment (Probability vs. Consequence)**
   - Severe thunderstorm probability is 45% precisely during the 6:00 PM - 9:00 PM event window.
   - Outdoor thunderstorms with 300 guests present severe guest safety risks, electrical hazards, catering ruin, and total event cancellation.

2. **Step 2: Capacity Feasibility Analysis (Option 2 Disqualification)**
   - Moving indoors to the backup ballroom has a hard capacity of 260 seats.
   - With 300 confirmed attendees, choosing Option 2 guarantees that 40 guests (including sponsors and VIPs) will have no seating or must be turned away. This introduces an unacceptable brand and public relations catastrophe.

3. **Step 3: Cost-Benefit Analysis of Option 1 (Tenting)**
   - Cost: ,500.
   - Coverage: Fully covers 300 seated guests on the lawn, protects rented stage, audio equipment, and formal dining decor.
   - Insurance Value: ,500 represents less than 10-15% of a typical 300-person gala budget to guarantee operational continuity.

4. **Step 4: Operational Action Timeline**
   - **10:00 AM:** Monitor updated high-resolution radar and consult site coordinator.
   - **12:30 PM:** Pre-decision weather check with meteorology service.
   - **1:00 PM (Hard Cutoff):** Authorize tenting installation (,500) if precipitation probability remains >25%.
   - **2:00 PM - 6:00 PM:** Tent construction and sidewall staging on lawn.
   - **6:00 PM:** Final sound/lighting sign-off under covered canopy.
